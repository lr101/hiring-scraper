Task: tailor the CV to the posting. Preserve verified employers, titles, dates and section
order. Identify the core work the employer is hiring for and map it to the strongest evidence.

Headline and profile
- Write a one-line headline of 45–65 characters (maximum 70). Use two or three truthful
  functional pillars ordered by fit. Use the posting's role-family wording for a verified
  functional area, without presenting the target job title as a past title. Include coordination when the posting asks for it and
  substantial verified work supports it. Use job titles, role families and domains only at
  verified scope. Never promote a one-off study project into a headline specialty. Cite
  each pillar's supporting evidence.
- Fill `profile.first` and `profile.second` with one sentence each. Use `profile.third`
  only for a distinct core duty with strong evidence, otherwise return null. Aim for roughly
  35–55 words in total. Plan the paragraph as a connected argument about the posting's
  core work: lead with a verified professional identity, follow with the strongest matching
  work evidence, and add a distinct technical or analytical strength if useful. When the
  applicant has work experience, lead with the profession, without graduate framing or
  opening on a study project. Keep each sentence independently cited and connect them
  through the progression of ideas. Summarize what the experience demonstrates instead
  of repeating any bullet's action-and-result wording.
- German: implicit first person, with no explicit "ich" and no third-person role subject
  ("Die Ingenieurin …" is wrong). Start with a noun-led CV opening such as "Ingenieurin
  für … mit Erfahrung in …". Follow with direct, subject-less verb-led clauses as natural.
- English: use a noun-led opening and fluent CV sentences with an implied applicant where
  natural. Avoid sentence fragments, bare infinitives and explicit applicant pronouns.
- Every sentence has its own `sources`, supporting the text of that sentence alone.
  Each sentence may draw on one entry and general identity or skill evidence. Never
  borrow context from another sentence or another title. Use a study fact only as study
  experience, explicitly naming it as a study
  project where relevant. A company in a project label is a topic, not an employer, client,
  or project recipient unless the cited fact explicitly says so. Never attach a job title to
  work in a different entry. If two sources describe unrelated actions, state them
  independently or use separate sentences. Never invent a relationship between them.
- Leave language levels to their dedicated section and motivation to the letter.

Entries (one slot per base bullet)
- In ENTRIES each bullet has a source id and its original text. Within each entry, order all
  bullet ids by relevance to the posting's core duties. Put the clearest evidence first.
- Assign `relevance` to every bullet. Plan on 2–4 high slots across the CV where there is
  that much direct evidence. A high slot directly evidences a core posting duty and benefits
  from a wording change. Rewrite each high slot, returning `text` and its exact `source`.
  Use medium for adjacent evidence or bullets already phrased in the posting's vocabulary,
  and low for unrelated work. Return only `relevance` for medium and low; the application
  keeps those bullets verbatim. Entries without bullets have no response slot.
- Rewrite from the one base bullet in that slot. Return its complete text with the
  smallest equivalent term substituted where it exposes the duty. Keep the original
  verb strength and clause order, adjusting grammar as needed. Vary opening verbs across
  rewrites with equivalents at the same strength, or keep a bullet verbatim when a change
  adds little. Keep every action the posting asks for, even if it is a secondary clause
  (for example, retain verified market research alongside prototyping). Leave actors, objects, recipients, tools, scope and measured
  results intact. Keep separate source facts separate even within one bullet.
  If the source says a person analyzed results and separately discussed improvements with
  colleagues, never say they discussed those results with the colleagues.
- Surface the posting's vocabulary where it describes the exact same action or method:
  e.g. cost analysis for costing, product training for training on a product, and iterative
  work for documented Kanban iterations. Distinguish analyzing KPIs from making a
  data-driven decision, and requirements prioritization from deciding the roadmap.
  Preserve early-stage scope and study context. Leave a term unchanged if its posting
  counterpart adds a different object, stage, relationship, or level of responsibility.
- Keep rewrites as short as the source or shorter without dropping substantive evidence.
  This CV must fit two pages. The application retains project labels; write only the body
  in `text`, keeping each study project as one bullet.

Skills
