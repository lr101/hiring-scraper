Task: tailor the CV to the posting. Preserve verified employers, titles, dates and section
order. Identify the core work the employer is hiring for and map it to the strongest evidence.

Headline and profile
- Write a one-line headline of at most 70 characters, preferably 45–65. Build it from 2–3
  verified functional areas the applicant has actually worked in, chosen and ordered for
  closest relevance to the posting (for example,
  "Produktentwicklung | Projekt- & Schnittstellenkoordination | CAD & Kalkulation" or
  "Product Development | Requirements & Prototyping | Stakeholder Alignment").
  Never use a role-family label or job title that the applicant's verified titles and work
  do not hold (do not use "Product Management" / "Produktmanagement" without formal PM
  tenure). Every pillar must rest on substantial or recurring verified work — never elevate
  a one-off activity from a single student project into a standalone headline pillar. Keep
  each phrase idiomatic and complete (e.g. "Early Product Development", not "Early
  Development"). Add a domain such as software or SaaS only if directly supported.
- Return exactly one profile unit made of 2–3 short, distinct sentences (never one long
  run-on sentence; aim for roughly 35–55 words total). Open the first sentence with a noun
  phrase naming the applicant's verified professional identity or degree (use it once, in
  the first sentence only). Synthesize the applicant's verified cross-cutting strengths
  against the posting's core duties — such as verified product development methods and tools
  (CAD, BOM/costing, prototyping, KPI analysis, iterative workflows), cross-functional,
  supplier, or developer collaboration, training design with its verified quantified result,
  external partnerships, and a verified language level when the posting explicitly requires
  that language (weave it smoothly into one of the 2–3 sentences rather than adding a choppy
  4-word standalone sentence). Do NOT repeat any experience or project bullet's wording and do NOT retell
  an individual employer or student project in detail (keep project specifics in their entry
  bullets). Keep active, concrete phrasing with no explicit first-person (I/ich) or
  third-person (she/he/sie/er) pronouns, no bare verb-first openings, and no agentless
  passives. Avoid repeating a content noun three times. Save motivation for the letter.
- Cite every evidence id supporting each capability, tool, quantified result, or language
  level in the profile. Keep any evidence gap visible in the scope of the wording.

Entries
- For each listed entry, retain and reorder its bullets by relevance to the posting's CORE
  responsibilities, including education projects. When an entry has a clear ownership or
  scope bullet that matches a core duty, place that bullet first so the reader immediately
  sees the scope before supporting details. Order education projects by relevance to the
  posting (for example, lead with a verified costing/BOM project for a role involving cost
  analysis, or with a verified research-to-prototype project for a software role).
- Give the strongest entries concrete bridges to duties explicitly named in the posting by
  showing verified comparable actions and results. Use truthful posting keywords where cited
  evidence supports them: agile or iterative for verified Kanban or iterative delivery,
  product discovery for verified user/market research and concept validation, working with
  developers on technical feasibility where supported, data-driven for verified KPI analysis,
  market analysis for verified market research, and cost analysis for verified costing. Name
  the underlying verified method and keep its scope exact. Cite the original bullet for every
  rewrite and any career evidence that supplies further verified detail. Use only evidence
  from the same employer or project.
- Every base bullet stays, including older roles, internship bullets and every education
  project. Return one output bullet per source in each entry's bullet bank. Put the matching
  base bullet citation first. Combine bullets only when they describe the same action and
  outcome with the same details, and cite both base bullets in that case.
- Fit the whole CV within two pages. Save space with direct verbs, short clauses and precise
  objects. When responsibilities overlap, give each bullet its own focus and keep the
  distinct details from its source.
- Start each bullet with a direct past-tense active verb and concrete object (no explicit
  first-person OR third-person pronouns referring to the applicant — never write "I", "ich",
  "she", "he", "die sie koordinierte", etc.). Hard cap: NO opening verb may start more than
  two bullets across the entire CV (track across all work and education entries). Also avoid
  overusing the same verb inside multiple bullets when more exact active verbs fit.
  - In German, rotate direct active verbs (e.g. Erarbeitete, Analysierte, Konzipierte,
    Koordinierte, Steuerte, Begleitete, Erstellte, Strukturierte, Moderierte, Prüfte,
    Übersetzte, Entwickelte, Organisierte, Überarbeitete, Pflegte, Beriet, Stimmte … ab);
    avoid awkward verb-noun phrases like "Leitete die Koordination" (write "Koordinierte"),
    needless reflexive "sich" constructions, clunky multi-noun compounds, repeated word stems
    within a bullet, and abrupt endings. For joint work, use "Wirkte an … mit" or "Trug zu …
    bei". Use suspension hyphens in coordinated compounds (e.g. "Markt- und Nutzerbedürfnisse").
  - In English, rotate direct active verbs (e.g. Led, Directed, Defined, Translated, Mapped,
    Coordinated, Facilitated, Prototyped, Evaluated, Structured, Prepared, Synthesized,
    Managed, Delivered, Revised, Built) and avoid redundant verb-noun phrases like "Led
    coordination of" (write "Directed" or "Coordinated"). Rewrite passive filler lines (such
    as "Explored emerging technologies…") into the concrete verified action, domain, or
    deliverable from the cited source.
- Preserve exact authorship and ownership: if the source says the applicant revised SOPs or
  built a guide, state that they revised or built it — never weaken it to merely "using" it.
- Aim for 16–24 words for most bullets and 22–32 for project bullets that need context. Use
  parallel objects and concise clauses. Retain distinguishing details (named collaborators,
  languages, tools, deliverables, suppliers, scope, verified metrics) once, where they best
  substantiate that responsibility. Preserve every verified quantified result with the action
  it measures, even when shortening or merging bullets. When the posting calls for sales
  enablement, customer training, or working with partners, surface the corresponding verified
  deliverables and collaborator details. Never add unsupported qualifiers ("technical"/"technisch",
  "strategic", "software", etc.) not present in the cited source.
- Use one self-contained bullet per labelled project. Preserve a short project lead_in ending
  with a colon and merge related details into that bullet. Do not follow a labelled project
  bullet with an unlabelled continuation from the same source. Use an empty lead_in for ordinary work bullets.
- Keep degree names in education headings. Do not repeat the degree in project bullets.

Skills
- Tailor competencies and tools deliberately: return existing unit ids in strict relevance
  order in `unit_ids` (for example, place BOM & costing skills right near the front for a
  role involving cost analysis, and agile/prototyping/KPI items first for a software product
  role). Keep verified foundational skills qualified and self-contained (e.g. "(Basics)" /
  "(Grundlagen)"), ordered by relevance rather than buried at the end when they match a core
  duty. Keep tools explicitly named in visible bullets. Use `hidden_ids` sparingly, only for
  clearly irrelevant items.
- Use `additions` for up to 4 short (1–4 words maximum) evidence-backed bridge tags across the
  CV, or an empty list. Build each label from concrete terms in the cited evidence. Use a
  posting term when that wording is supported by the evidence. Cite every supporting evidence
  id. Omit duplicates and use the verified method or deliverable as the label.
