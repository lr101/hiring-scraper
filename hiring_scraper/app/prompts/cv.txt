Task: tailor the CV to the posting. Preserve verified employers, titles, dates and section
order. Identify the core work the employer is hiring for and map it to the strongest evidence.

Headline and profile
- Write a one-line headline of at most 70 characters, preferably 45–60. Put the posting's
  role-family keyword first when verified work is adjacent to that family. Frame it as a
  functional focus, without implying a held title, seniority or ownership. Use the closest
  truthful term if the role-family keyword would overstate the evidence. Add up to two
  short, complementary focuses supported by the evidence. For example, for a Product Manager
  posting with verified product discovery, requirements prioritization, or coordination of
  early product development, use "Product Management" (German "Produktmanagement") as a
  functional focus. Qualify it with the verified stage or focus, and add a domain such as
  software or SaaS only if supported.
- Return exactly one profile unit with 2–3 concise sentences. Open with a noun phrase naming
  the applicant's verified professional identity or degree. Use active constructions with
  the applicant as the actor (for example, "Engineer who mapped …" or "Ingenieurin, die …
  entwickelte"). No explicit I/ich or she/he/sie/er, bare verb-first openings, or agentless
  passives. Use the degree or professional identity once, in the first sentence only. Name
  one relevant project or deliverable, or two if each proves a different core responsibility.
  Mention each once, even if its entry falls on page 2. Give just the detail needed to show
  what the applicant can do for this role. Avoid miniature case studies, repeated job or
  degree labels, and separate project lists. No noun may occur three times, including
  singular/plural variants. Save motivation for the letter.
- Cite the actual work supporting each claim. If the profile names a past employer, cite a
  bullet or intro from that entry. Keep any evidence gap visible in the scope of the wording.

Entries
- For each listed entry, select and reorder its bullets by relevance to the posting's CORE
  responsibilities, including education projects. Give the strongest entries concrete bridges
  to duties explicitly named in the posting by showing a verified comparable action and result.
  Use posting keywords where they accurately describe cited work: agile or iterative for verified
  Kanban or iterations, data-driven for work using KPI analysis, market analysis for verified
  market research, and cost analysis for verified costing. Name the underlying method and
  keep its scope exact. Cite the original bullet for every rewrite and any career evidence
  that supplies further verified detail. Lead with the work closest to the hiring responsibilities rather than the biggest result in a less relevant area. Use only
  evidence from the same employer or project.
- Allocate bullets by relevance: give the most relevant role at least as many bullets as less
  relevant roles, and condense less relevant 4+ bullet roles to 2–3 focused bullets so page 1
  holds more high-relevance content. Omit low-relevance filler. For entries with fewer than
  4 base bullets, stay within one fewer to one more than the base count (at least one).
  For education or older roles outside the target domain, use the low end of this range
  unless each additional bullet proves a distinct core duty in the posting. Favor a
  transferable method that fits the work over an impressive but unrelated deliverable.
- Merge overlapping duties within an entry: give each bullet a distinct responsibility, action
  and purpose. Before writing, assign each bullet a separate topic and put overlapping facts
  (such as coordination, requirements, stakeholder feedback or documentation) in one place
  rather than splitting them across two bullets. Never repeat a phrase of four or more words
  between bullets in the same entry. Use a recurring short term only if needed for precision.
- Start each bullet with a direct past-tense active verb and concrete object (no explicit
  first-person OR third-person pronouns referring to the applicant — never write "I", "ich",
  "she", "he", "die sie koordinierte", etc.). Vary opening verbs across the entire CV: no
  opening verb may start more than 2 bullets in the whole CV.
  - In German, rotate direct active verbs (e.g. Erarbeitete, Analysierte, Konzipierte,
    Koordinierte, Steuerte, Begleitete, Erstellte, Strukturierte, Moderierte, Prüfte,
    Übersetzte, Entwickelte, Organisierte, Stimmte … ab); avoid awkward verb-noun phrases like
    "Leitete die Koordination" (write "Koordinierte") and avoid needless reflexive "sich"
    constructions. For joint work, use "Wirkte an … mit" or "Trug zu … bei". Use suspension
    hyphens in coordinated compounds (e.g. "Markt- und Nutzerbedürfnisse").
  - In English, rotate direct active verbs (e.g. Defined, Translated, Mapped, Coordinated,
    Facilitated, Prototyped, Evaluated, Structured, Prepared, Synthesized, Managed, Delivered).
- Aim for 18–28 words for most bullets and 25–40 for project bullets that need context. Use
  parallel objects and concise clauses. Retain distinguishing details (named collaborators,
  languages, tools, deliverables, suppliers, scope, verified metrics) once, where they best
  substantiate that responsibility. Keep a relevant documented outcome and the activity that
  produced it together. When the posting calls for sales enablement, customer training, or
  working with partners, surface the corresponding verified deliverables and collaborator
  details. Never add unsupported qualifiers ("technical"/"technisch",
  "strategic", "software", etc.) not present in the cited source.
- Use one self-contained bullet per labelled project. Preserve a short project lead_in ending
  with a colon and merge related details into that bullet. Do not follow a labelled project
  bullet with an unlabelled continuation. Use an empty lead_in for ordinary work bullets.
- Keep degree names in education headings. Do not repeat the degree in project bullets.

Skills
- Tailor competencies deliberately: return existing unit ids in relevance order in `unit_ids`
  and put clearly irrelevant or low-value items in `hidden_ids` so the section stays sharp and
  targeted. Keep relevant basic skills qualified, self-contained, and last.
- Use `additions` for up to 4 short (1–4 words maximum) evidence-backed bridge tags across the
  CV, or an empty list. Never write long phrases. Each tag must use at least one substantive
  term from the posting and draw the rest from the cited applicant evidence (compound nouns
  built from supported evidence terms are valid). Cite every supporting evidence id. Omit
  synonyms or duplicates of existing competencies.
