Task: tailor the CV to the posting. Preserve verified employers, titles, dates and section
order. Identify the core work the employer is hiring for and map it to the strongest evidence.

Headline and profile
- Write a one-line headline of at most 70 characters, preferably 45–60. Use 2–3 pipe-separated
  functional focuses in the posting's role family backed strictly by the evidence (e.g.
  "Product Development | Requirements & Prototyping | Stakeholder Coordination" style). Never
  overstate the domain (e.g. never write "Software Development" when the evidence is product or
  requirements coordination) and never claim an unverified job title, seniority, tenure or
  ownership.
- Write a noun-phrase-led profile summary (unit 1 of `profile`) of 3–4 sentences (~55–80 words)
  with no explicit first person (no I, I'm, I've or ich) and no imperatives or clipped
  verb-first fragments.
  - Sentence 1 MUST begin with a noun phrase naming the applicant's verified professional
    identity or degree from the evidence (e.g. "<Degree> engineer with …" /
    "<Abschluss>-Ingenieurin mit …"). Never start sentence 1 with a verb.
  - Subsequent sentences may use complete sentences or noun-phrase/participial constructions,
    never bare imperatives or conjugated verb-first fragments (never start a sentence with
    "Bringe", "Verfüge", "Verbinde", "Combine", "Turn", "Lead", etc.).
  - Surface the 2–3 pieces of evidence most relevant to the posting's CORE responsibilities right
    on page 1, even when that work appears in education or projects on page 2. Synthesize
    across evidence instead of paraphrasing a bullet. Save motivation for the letter.
- When 2–3 highly relevant projects or deliverables sit in education or later sections, add a
  compact second unit in `profile` starting with "Selected projects:" (English) or
  "Relevante Projekte:" (German) naming them concisely with their evidence `sources`. Otherwise
  return one profile unit.
- Cite the actual work supporting each claim. If the profile names a past employer, cite a
  bullet or intro from that entry. Keep any evidence gap visible in the scope of the wording.

Entries
- For each listed entry, select and reorder its bullets by relevance to the posting's CORE
  responsibilities, including education projects. Cite the original bullet for every rewrite and
  any career evidence that supplies further verified detail. Lead with the work closest to the
  hiring responsibilities rather than the biggest result in a less relevant area. Use only
  evidence from the same employer or project.
- Allocate bullets by relevance: give the most relevant role at least as many bullets as less
  relevant roles, and condense less relevant 4+ bullet roles to 2–3 focused bullets so page 1
  holds more high-relevance content. For entries with fewer than 4 base bullets, stay within
  one fewer to one more than the base count (at least one).
- Merge overlapping duties within an entry: give each bullet a distinct responsibility, action
  and purpose. Before writing, assign each bullet a separate topic and put overlapping facts
  (such as coordination, requirements, stakeholder feedback or documentation) in one place
  rather than splitting them across two bullets.
- Start each bullet with a direct past-tense active verb and concrete object (no explicit
  first-person OR third-person pronouns referring to the applicant — never write "I", "ich",
  "she", "he", "die sie koordinierte", etc.). Vary opening verbs across the entire CV: no
  opening verb may start more than 2 bullets in the whole CV.
  - In German, rotate direct active verbs (e.g. Erarbeitete, Analysierte, Konzipierte,
    Koordinierte, Steuerte, Begleitete, Erstellte, Strukturierte, Moderierte, Prüfte,
    Übersetzte, Entwickelte, Organisierte, Stimmte … ab); avoid awkward verb-noun phrases like
    "Leitete die Koordination" (write "Koordinierte") and avoid needless reflexive "sich"
    constructions. For joint work, use "Wirkte an … mit" or "Trug zu … bei". Use suspension
    hyphens in coordinated compounds (e.g. "Markt- und Nutzerbedürfnisse").
  - In English, rotate direct active verbs (e.g. Defined, Translated, Mapped, Coordinated,
    Facilitated, Prototyped, Evaluated, Structured, Prepared, Synthesized, Managed, Delivered).
- Aim for 18–28 words for most bullets and 25–40 for project bullets that need context. Use
  parallel objects and concise clauses. Retain distinguishing details (named collaborators,
  languages, tools, deliverables, suppliers, scope, verified metrics) once, where they best
  substantiate that responsibility. Never add unsupported qualifiers ("technical"/"technisch",
  "strategic", "software", etc.) not present in the cited source.
- Use one self-contained bullet per labelled project. Preserve a short project lead_in ending
  with a colon and merge related details into that bullet. Do not follow a labelled project
  bullet with an unlabelled continuation. Use an empty lead_in for ordinary work bullets.
- Keep degree names in education headings. Do not repeat the degree in project bullets.

Skills
- Tailor competencies deliberately: return existing unit ids in relevance order in `unit_ids`
  and put clearly irrelevant or low-value items in `hidden_ids` so the section stays sharp and
  targeted.
- Use `additions` for up to 4 short (1–4 words maximum) evidence-backed bridge tags across the
  CV, or an empty list. Never write long phrases. Each tag must use at least one substantive
  term from the posting and draw the rest from the cited applicant evidence (compound nouns
  built from supported evidence terms are valid). Cite every supporting evidence id. Omit
  synonyms or duplicates of existing competencies.
