Task: write a cover letter for the specific work in the posting. The application fills in
sender, recipient, date, salutation, sign-off and signature.

Structure
- Write three body paragraphs and a short closing by default. Add a fourth body paragraph
  only if it proves another core requirement or offers a specific application of a verified
  method. Aim for 270–310 words in English or 260–320 in German, including the closing.
- Motivation: connect a duty stated in the posting to a concrete piece of verified work.
  State interest directly and warmly in the applicant's voice; use the company's name in the
  opening and, if natural, in the closing. Ground any description of the employer in the
  posting. Save the details of the example for its body paragraph.
- Examples: choose and order examples strictly by the posting's core duties. In each
  experience or education paragraph, name one core duty and connect it to the closest
  verified example:
  - For a software or SaaS product role, prefer verified software platform development,
    collaboration with developers on technical feasibility and iterative/agile delivery,
    KPI/data work paired with a verified operational result (such as an onboarding-time
    reduction), and research-to-prototype work over unrelated physical-product concepts.
  - For a hardware or industrial product role, prefer verified CAD/BOM/costing work,
    market or benchmark analysis, partner or customer collaboration, and training delivery.
  Add a verified decision, method or result the CV leaves out. When evidence is limited to
  the CV, explain how the verified work transfers to the named duty. Use each employer or
  education project once, name the discipline for study examples, and keep each paragraph
  to three or four sentences.
- Language requirement: when the posting explicitly requires a language (for example,
  English or German) and the evidence verifies a strong level, weave the verified level
  naturally into a substantive body sentence (for instance alongside verified international
  collaboration or working in that language). Never omit a required verified language level
  and never tack it on as an isolated sentence. Cite the language evidence alongside the
  collaboration evidence.
- Use role "bring" only for a distinct, concrete application of a past method to a specific
  posting task. Cite job and supporting profile/skill evidence.
- Closing: end with a brief thank-you and a warm, natural invitation to a personal
  conversation or interview in the applicant's voice (matching the tone of the voice
  exemplars). Keep this concise and conversational. NEVER introduce a new factual claim,
  project detail, or technical assertion in the closing — all qualifications and examples
  belong in the body.

Check the final draft before returning it
- Assign each employer or education project to one paragraph only. Check the cited CV entry
  ids, including in the motivation and bring paragraphs. Even different ids from one employer
  belong in one place. Cite the original evidence behind claims drawn from the CV.
- Read across paragraphs for repeated wording. Use fresh verbs and clauses for CV facts. No
  eight consecutive words may match the CV. Avoid repeating the posting's title or stock
  bridge phrases. Keep distinctive facts and short identifiers where they matter.
- Formula and rhythm checks:
  - Use at most ONE forward-looking conditional formula ("würde ich …" / "I would bring …" /
    "I would apply …") in the ENTIRE letter; use indicative, varied constructions everywhere
    else.
  - Never end two body paragraphs with the same closing construction or bridge formula (do
    not repeat replacement formulas like "I am keen to …" across paragraphs either), and
    reserve "I look forward to" / "Ich freue mich auf" for the closing sentence only so no
    phrase repeats between a body paragraph and the closing.
  - Never start three or more consecutive sentences with "I <verb>" or the same opening
    pattern; vary openings by leading with context, purpose, tools, collaborators or outcomes.
  - In English, use "KPIs" rather than "performance indicators".
- Verify the action, method and outcome separately against cited evidence. Match leadership
  scope, stage and results exactly. Check causal links too: when evidence supports two
  contributions separately, write them as independent actions. Do not imply one produced or
  informed the other unless the source says so. Do not claim unsupported tenure, releases,
  or decisions. Include "job" in sources whenever a paragraph names the target company, its
  product, or a duty from the posting, and use "job" for facts about the posting only.
- Spell out English contractions. In German, keep formal Sie-Form throughout ("Sie"/"Ihr",
  matching the voice notes) and start lowercase after the comma-ended salutation unless the
  first word is a noun, proper name or formal Sie/Ihr.

Use the role in "subject", with a reference number only if the posting explicitly provides it.
In "contact_name", copy the name explicitly identified as the vacancy contact, without titles
or honorifics. In English, use the first name alone if that is all the posting gives. In German,
return a full name only. Return an empty string for an email-only or unnamed contact. Do not
use a name mentioned only as a colleague or inferred from an email address.
