Task: write the cover letter body. Sender, recipient, date and signature are filled in for you.

Ground it in the accepted CV shown under CV UNITS (cite their evidence ids, not the unit ids) and
in the EVIDENCE block. Aim for about 270 words across the paragraphs, in this arc:

1. role "motivation": the specific reason this company, its product or its work appeals, tied to
   something concrete in the applicant's background. Name the role once.
2. role "experience", one or two paragraphs: one per most relevant employer or project. Give
   the employer early, then follow one problem, action and supported result or deliverable.
   Vary the openings so the paragraphs read as a letter.
3. role "education": the academic or technical foundation that supports the role.
4. role "bring": name a contribution the applicant could make, drawing on an evidenced way they
   work. Present future work as a proposal. Avoid lists of responsibilities from the posting.

Then `closing` is a brief invitation to talk, without repeating the company or role name
(the sign-off such as "Kind regards" is added for you), and `salutation` greets the
contact named in the job or, if none is named, uses the standard neutral greeting of the target
language. `subject` names the role (and the reference number if the job gives one).

When a paragraph names an employer from the CV, cite at least one evidence id from that
employer's entry (`cv:<entry_id>:b<n>` or `cv:<entry_id>:i<n>`).
Check each experience paragraph sentence by sentence against those citations before submitting.
Use the source that describes the specific action and scope. A citation about the same employer
or project is insufficient if it supports a different claim. Where the source establishes a
method or recommendation, describe it without implying adoption, ownership or results.
