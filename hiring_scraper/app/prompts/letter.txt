Task: write a cover letter tailored to the specific work in the posting. Sender, recipient,
date, salutation, sign-off and signature are filled in by the application.

Write three or four paragraphs in `paragraphs` and one brief closing unit in `closing`, for
four or five body paragraphs in total. Target about 270–310 body words in English or 260–320
body words in German (including `closing`).

- Open with one motivation paragraph. Do NOT open by explaining the employer's own product back
  to them; in the first sentence, connect their specific product, mission or problem directly to
  the applicant's verified background. Describe the employer's product, customers and role
  strictly from the posting's wording — never invent capabilities or customer segments not
  stated in the posting. Skip tenuous personal connections, origin stories and autobiography.
  State interest directly in the present tense.
- Select the two strongest examples for the posting's core responsibilities by default. Add a
  third only when it proves a separate core responsibility that the first two do not address.
  If the strongest prior work has a different scope or stage, use the relevant steps of that
  work rather than a less relevant example from a different domain. Use one paragraph per
  employer or project. Combine all details from any one CV entry into that paragraph and cite
  its cv:<entry_id>:b<n> or cv:<entry_id>:i<n> ids. Do not cite that entry again in a different
  paragraph. Additional career evidence can supplement it. Use experience or education for these
  paragraphs.
- Develop each example in flowing prose. Start with the situation or decision, describe the
  applicant's contribution at its exact verified scope (e.g. "led coordination of early-stage
  development", never overstating to "led early development"), and connect EVERY experience or
  education paragraph back to the target work or role. When an education paragraph refers to
  the applicant's studies, name the applicant's verified degree or discipline naturally. Vary
  sentence openings (never start more than two consecutive sentences in a paragraph with "I "
  or "Ich ") and **vary paragraph endings** — never end two paragraphs with the same closing
  formula or identical opening words (e.g. never start two paragraph-ending sentences with
  "Diese Erfahrung …" or "This experience …"). Explain why the actions fit together instead of
  retelling the CV in order. For projects outside the employer's domain, lead with the relevant
  method or decision and give only enough context to understand it.
- Use fresh wording even for the same evidence. No eight consecutive words may match any CV
  unit. Keep distinctive details intact while changing the focus and sentence structure.
- If the evidence does not show a requirement such as a specific tenure or ownership through
  release, do not imply it. Where useful, one honest sentence can frame transferable experience
  and the stage at which the applicant earned it. Include relevant verified qualifications such
  as language proficiency in the example where they matter, without tacking them on after a
  paragraph's final connection to the target work.
- If a fourth paragraph (`bring`) adds something beyond the examples, ground the proposed
  approach in a **verified past method** the applicant has used (such as user research,
  workflow mapping, stakeholder interviews, requirements structuring or prototyping) rather
  than a generic hypothetical, and apply it concretely to a specific task from the posting at
  the applicant's verified scope. Cite `job` and supporting profile/skill evidence (do not
  re-cite a CV entry already used in an earlier paragraph). Prefer indicative voice and use at
  most one conditional (würde or would) in any paragraph.
- Register and mechanics:
  - In English, spell out contractions.
  - In German, **always use formal Sie-Form** (Sie/Ihnen/Ihr), even when the posting uses
    informal "du". Because the salutation ends with a comma, start the first word of the first
    paragraph with a lowercase letter unless it is a German noun or formal Sie/Ihr.

Cite evidence ids in EVIDENCE, including the original sources behind claims shown in CV UNITS.
Use `job` for facts established by the posting. Check sentence by sentence that the evidence
supports the action, method and outcome at the stated scope, with no unsupported qualifiers.
Keep recommendations distinct from decisions or adoption. Do not claim unverified work even
when closely related.

Use `closing` for a natural, warm closing line (optionally preceded by a brief thank-you, e.g.
"Vielen Dank für Ihre Zeit. Ich freue mich auf ein persönliches Gespräch." / "Ich freue mich
auf ein persönliches Gespräch." in German, or "Thank you for your time and consideration. I
look forward to discussing the role with you." in English). Name the role in `subject`; include
a reference number only if the posting text explicitly states one. For `contact_name`, copy the
full name (first and last) of the person the posting identifies as the contact for this vacancy,
without any title or honorific. Return an empty string if only a first name or an email is
given, or if there is no named contact.
