Task: write a cover letter for the specific work in the posting. The application fills in
sender, recipient, date, salutation, sign-off and signature.

Output contract
- EXAMPLE SLOTS contains original evidence grouped by employer or institution (including
  organization, title, location and facts). Select the best two or three distinct slots
  for the posting's core duties. Each slot takes one paragraph or null. Set priority 1 for
  the most relevant paragraph and number the rest in reading order. Give each selected slot
  an anchor from the single entry described in that paragraph.
- Write each example as linked sentences. Assign every sentence one source that verifies
  the exact action, collaborator and outcome stated. Use `job` only for a sentence about
  the posting or an explicitly future contribution. Use `profile:language_levels` when a
  sentence integrates a required verified language level. Use the slot's supplemental facts
  only when they concern the chosen entry. If no single source supports the complete
  sentence, split or narrow it. The application joins the sentences and retains their ids.
- `motivation` (`text` + `sources`): open with specific interest in the posting's work.
  The opening paragraph may ONLY make experience or capability claims that cite exact source
  ids in `motivation.sources`; use `["job"]` if it states interest in the role without past
  claims. Save detailed past actions, collaborators and results for the example slots.
- `fit` (`text` + `sources`, or `null`): use for a distinct closing argument (such as a
  short clause on a required verified language level + international work from an entry not
  already used as an example slot, or a focused future contribution). Cite every source id
  used (including `profile:language_levels` when mentioning a required language level, and
  `job` for future-oriented statements). Use `fit=null` when it adds no distinct point. Use
  at most four body paragraphs including `motivation` and `fit`; use fewer when evidence is
  sparse.

Structure
- Write three body paragraphs and a short closing by default. Add a fourth body paragraph
  when a core requirement otherwise lacks a verified example. Aim for 270–310 words in
  English or 260–320 in German, including the closing.
- Motivation: connect a core duty stated in the posting directly and warmly to the
  applicant's interest (and, if cited in `motivation.sources`, a concrete verified
  capability). Use the company's name in the opening and, if natural, in the closing. Ground
  every statement about the employer strictly in the posting — never speculate about the
  employer's internal data, tools, or processes that the posting does not state. Use exact
  team names, product names and role titles as written in JOB.
- Examples: choose and order example slots strictly by the posting's core duties. In each
  experience or education paragraph, name one core duty and connect it to the closest
  verified example:
  - For a software or SaaS product role, centre the letter on the software platform work
    (prioritization with developers, Figma prototypes, Kanban iterations, and how
    stakeholder feedback changed scope or priorities where documented). Keep unrelated
    consumer-product study work to at most one sentence — or omit it if stronger software,
    platform or operations evidence fills the slots.
  - When the posting lists training or enablement duties and the evidence has a verified
    training or onboarding result, include that verified result.
  - For a hardware or industrial product role, prefer verified CAD/BOM/costing work,
    market or benchmark analysis, and partner or customer collaboration. Use verified
    external collaboration for a core partnership duty, keeping the actual partner type
    and nature of the collaboration.
  - When the posting requires a language and/or travel or international collaboration,
    include one short, natural clause combining the verified language level (only when that
    language is explicitly required by the posting) with verified international work or
    study experience at its documented scope. Keep the clause out of unrelated outcomes.
  Where the evidence links feedback or analysis to a change in scope, priorities or a
  decision, show what the applicant did with that feedback or result. Where the source gives
  only an action and purpose, use those. Name the discipline for study examples. Keep each
  paragraph to three or four sentences.
- Closing: combine a brief thank-you with a specific invitation to a personal conversation
  or interview in one warm, concise sentence (or two short sentences), naming a concrete
  topic from the role that the applicant wants to discuss. Introduce no new claims or
  qualifications.

Check the final draft before returning it
- Write fresh first-person active narrative prose throughout — do NOT restate CV bullets
  or copy evidence wording verbatim. Paraphrase slot facts into natural narrative showing
  what the applicant did, how they worked with collaborators, and what changed from feedback
  or results. Give each paragraph a different duty or argument. Merge sentences that restate
  the same point, and avoid recycling an action/object phrase or bridge verb across examples.
- Formula and rhythm checks:
  - Keep active first-person voice throughout, while varying sentence openings so three or
    more consecutive sentences never start with "I <verb>" or the same opening pattern
    (lead some sentences with context, purpose, tools, collaborators or outcomes before the
    first-person subject).
  - Use at most ONE forward-looking conditional formula ("würde ich …" / "I would bring …" /
    "I would apply …") in the ENTIRE letter; use indicative, varied constructions everywhere
    else.
  - Never end two body paragraphs with the same closing construction or bridge formula (do
    not repeat replacement formulas like "I am keen to …" across paragraphs either), and
    reserve "I look forward to" / "Ich freue mich auf" for the closing sentence only so no
    phrase repeats between a body paragraph and the closing.
  - Once the connection to the role is clear, land the paragraph on a verified action,
    outcome or specific question. Avoid speculative bridges about what the employer's
    internal data or systems look like.
  - In English, use "KPIs" rather than "performance indicators".
- Audit every past-tense clause and every statement about the applicant's usual practice
  against cited evidence. Keep verb strength, actors, recipients, leadership scope, stage
  and results exact. Keep tracking, analyzing, proposing and deciding distinct. Where the
  evidence stops short of the posting duty, say what the applicant wants to do or learn in
  the new role. Use one independently verifiable action and its documented outcome per
  sentence. Keep separately stated source facts independent; never imply one caused or
  informed another unless the source says so. Use `job` as the source of sentences about
  the posting and future plans.
- Spell out English contractions. In German, keep formal Sie-Form throughout ("Sie"/"Ihr",
  matching the voice notes) and start lowercase after the comma-ended salutation unless the
  first word is a noun, proper name or formal Sie/Ihr.

Use "Application for <job title>" in the English subject and "Bewerbung als <Titel>" in
the German subject.
In "contact_name", copy the name explicitly identified as the vacancy contact, without titles
or honorifics. In English, use the first name alone if that is all the posting gives. In German,
return a full name only. Return an empty string for an email-only or unnamed contact. Do not
use a name mentioned only as a colleague or inferred from an email address.
