Task: write a focused, one-page cover letter tailored to this posting's core duties. The
application fills in sender, recipient, date, salutation, sign-off, and signature.

Output contract
- `motivation` (`text` + `sources`): open with specific interest in this company and a named core
  duty from JOB, linked to a concrete verified capability (cite its source id plus `job`). Ground
  every statement about the employer strictly in JOB; use exact team, product, and role names.
- `examples` (one optional slot per employer or institution in EXAMPLE SLOTS): choose 2–3 distinct
  slots covering the posting's core duties, numbered by `priority` (1, 2, …) in reading order. For
  each selected slot, name the posting duty in `duty`, set `anchor` to a fact from that entry, and
  write 3–4 linked sentences (`sentences`), each citing one allowed `source` that verifies it (use
  `job` for role or future statements, `profile:language_levels` when integrating a required
  language level).
- `fit` (`text` + `sources`, or `null`): use only for a distinct brief closing argument (such as a
  required verified language level or international context from an unused entry, or a focused
  future contribution); otherwise return `null`. Keep total body paragraphs (`motivation` +
  selected `examples` + optional `fit`) to 3–4.
- `closing` (`text`): one warm, specific sentence inviting a conversation about the role, with no
  new claims. In German, close idiomatically (e.g. "Über ein persönliches Gespräch zu … freue ich
  mich.") and omit "Vielen Dank für Ihre Zeit." In English, close naturally (e.g. "I would welcome
  a conversation about …").
- `subject` (`text`): "Bewerbung als <Titel>" in German or "Application for <job title>" in
  English.
- `contact_name`: exact named vacancy contact from JOB without titles or honorifics (full name in
  German; first name alone is fine if that is all English gives); empty string if unnamed or
  email-only.

Writing principles
- Aim for roughly 260–320 words total (including closing) so the letter fits cleanly on one A4
  page.
- Write in the applicant's own voice (using VOICE notes and exemplars for tone and rhythm only) as
  fresh first-person active prose — do not copy CV bullets verbatim.
- In each evidence paragraph, focus on 1–2 strong verified actions and a documented outcome
  (quantified where supported), and end by making clear what that experience means for the target
  role's duty. State study projects explicitly as academic context.
- Vary sentence openings, verbs, and paragraph endings naturally; avoid repeating the same closing
  bridge formula across paragraphs.
- English: spell out all contractions (e.g. "I have", "do not"). German: keep formal Sie-Form
  ("Sie"/"Ihr") throughout, and start paragraph 1 lowercase after the comma-ended salutation
  unless the first word is a noun, proper name, or Sie/Ihr.
