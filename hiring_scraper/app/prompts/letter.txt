Task: write a cover letter tailored to the specific work in the posting. Sender, recipient,
date, salutation, sign-off and signature are filled in by the application.

Write three or four paragraphs in paragraphs and one sentence in closing, for four or five
body paragraphs in total. Aim for about 260–290 words including the closing sentence.

- Open with one motivation paragraph. Name what interests the applicant about the employer's
  actual product, customers or work and the problem the role helps solve. Use only capabilities
  and customer needs supported by the posting. Skip tenuous personal connections, origin stories
  and autobiography. State interest directly, in the present tense.
- Select the two strongest examples for the posting's core responsibilities by default. Add a
  third only when it proves a separate core responsibility that the first two do not address.
  If the strongest prior work has a different scope or stage, use the relevant steps of that
  work rather than a less relevant example from a different domain. Use
  one paragraph per employer or project. Combine all details from any one CV entry into
  that paragraph and cite its cv:<entry_id>:b<n> or cv:<entry_id>:i<n> ids. Do not cite
  that entry again in a different paragraph. Additional career evidence can supplement it.
  Use experience or education for these paragraphs.
- Develop each example in flowing prose. Start with the situation or decision, describe the
  applicant's contribution at its verified scope, and end every experience or education
  paragraph with one sentence about what that
  experience means for this job. Make the final sentence about the target work, using a
  specific task or decision. Vary sentence openings and avoid repeating a noun three times
  in one paragraph. Explain why the actions fit together, instead of retelling the CV in order.
  For projects outside the employer's domain, lead with the relevant method or decision and
  give only enough context to understand it. Leave degree names and ancillary facts to the CV.
- Use fresh wording even for the same evidence. No eight consecutive words may match any CV
  unit. Keep distinctive details intact while changing the focus and sentence structure.
- If the evidence does not show a requirement such as a specific tenure or ownership through
  release, do not imply it. Where useful, one honest sentence can frame transferable experience
  and the stage at which the applicant earned it. Include relevant verified qualifications
  such as language proficiency in the example where they matter, without tacking them on
  after its final connection to the target work.
- If a fourth paragraph adds something beyond the two examples, use bring to name one concrete work
  decision and what would inform it, at the scope the applicant can offer. Prefer indicative
  voice and use at most one conditional (würde or would) in any paragraph. In English, spell
  out contractions. In German, use formal register and continue correctly after the comma.

Cite evidence ids in EVIDENCE, including the original sources behind claims shown in CV UNITS.
Use job for facts established by the posting. Check sentence by sentence that the evidence
supports the action, method and outcome at the stated scope. Keep recommendations distinct
from decisions or adoption. Do not claim unverified work even when closely related.

Use closing for a single brief invitation to talk. Name the role in subject and include a
reference number if the posting gives one. For contact_name, copy the full name of the person
the posting identifies as the contact for this vacancy, without any title or honorific. Return
an empty string if only a first name or an email is given, or if there is no named contact.
