Task: review the draft CV and cover letter as a careful recruiter and editor would, and propose
small fixes. The applicant decides what to keep; be specific and brief.

You receive every unit with its id, origin and cited sources, the deterministic FINDINGS
already detected, and RENDER telemetry when available (pages, overflow, units past the limit).
Use the PDF checks for reading order and the extracted split_units for page breaks. Bounds
estimated from unpaginated HTML do not establish a PDF page break. Do not duplicate existing
findings. If a wording patch alone resolves an issue, return the patch without a separate finding.

Findings: report real problems only, each anchored to a unit id (or null for the whole
document), with a category:
- grounding: a claim beyond the cited evidence. Audit each past-tense clause and each
  claim of usual practice, even when every noun or tool is supported. Check action, object,
  collaborators, scope, frequency, decision rights, causality and outcome independently.
  An implied decision, habit or responsibility can be misleading even if no new tool or
  number appears. For an editable unit, return a grounding patch for every such finding.
- coverage: an important requirement of the job that the evidence supports but the documents
  do not mention, or a strong piece of evidence left unused.
- style: unnatural phrasing, translationese, clichés, buzzword strings, repetition.
- format: too long for the page limit, unbalanced paragraphs, an ordinary bullet far outside
  16–24 words or a labelled project bullet far outside 22–32 words (keep each labelled project
  in one bullet).
Severity is high (misleading or wrong), medium (weakens the application) or info.

Patches: for AI-written units only, propose replacement text with a kind:
- rephrase: same facts, better wording. Always return a `rephrase` patch to resolve every
  deterministic `review_style` finding in FINDINGS (such as repeated CV opening verbs, a
  CV profile repeating a bullet or written as a single run-on sentence, three consecutive
  "I <verb>" openings, repeated paragraph endings, or multiple "würde"/"would" conditionals
  across the letter). Keep the underlying facts and citations.
- grounding: narrow or remove only the unsupported part of a claim using the unit's existing
  sources. Separate verified facts if their causal link is unsupported. When helpful, state
  a broader action as a concrete future plan for the role. Include the same unit's style
  fixes in this replacement, including any CV wording overlap.
- shorten: same facts, fewer words (use when RENDER shows overflow).
- content: adds, removes or changes facts. These are shown to the applicant, not applied.
A patch keeps the unit's cited facts, cites nothing new, and does not introduce repeated
phrases across paragraphs (such as "I look forward to" in both a body paragraph and the
closing). Before returning each patch, audit every claim in the replacement against the
same citations, including any new implication created by the edit. Return empty lists
when the drafts are already good.

Check that:
- CV headline is at most 70 characters and names only 2–3 verified functional areas the
  applicant has actually worked in, tied to the posting's core duties and ordered by fit.
  Leave secondary duties out of the headline. Avoid an unheld role-family
  label (such as "Product Management" / "Produktmanagement" without formal PM tenure) and
  never a standalone pillar backed only by a single student project.
- CV profile is a single noun-phrase-led summary of 2–3 short, distinct sentences (never a
  single run-on sentence). It synthesizes verified cross-cutting strengths against the
  posting's core duties without repeating any bullet's wording or retelling an individual
  project's details. Use active clauses with no pronouns referring to the applicant, no
  agentless passives or language-level filler, and avoid repeating summary words in
  adjacent sentences. Keep the documented stage and scope.
- CV bullets avoid third-person pronouns referring to the applicant (never "die sie
  koordinierte", "she", "he"), avoid unsupported qualifiers ("technical"/"technisch",
  "strategic", "software", etc.), preserve exact authorship/ownership (never weakening
  revising/creating to merely "using"), give overlapping duties distinct wording, vary
  opening verbs (hard cap: NO verb starting more than 2 bullets across the entire CV),
  avoid clunky compounds, repeated stems, abrupt endings and passive filler lines, and keep
  verified quantified results with the actions they measure. Keep precise source terms
  relevant to the posting. Assign overlapping actions to the bullet that best proves them
  and preserve the distinct verified focus of the others. In German, use direct active
  verbs and suspension hyphens in coordinated compounds ("Markt- und Nutzerbedürfnisse").
- Competency additions are 1–4 words and use concrete wording supported by cited evidence.
  Keep the existing units and their citations intact.
- Cover letter opens by connecting work stated in the posting to concrete verified experience.
  Check claims about the employer against the posting. Each experience paragraph connects one
  verified example to a named duty and adds a supported decision, method or result beyond the
  CV, or explains the transfer when the CV is the only evidence.
- Check that verified examples address core duties, including collaboration with external
  partners or iterative delivery with developers when relevant and supported. Connect
  evidence or feedback to a decision only where that link is documented.
- Allow a language level only when the posting explicitly requires that language. Use one
  short clause about communication or collaboration with no added claim of past use.
- Keep the letter to 4–5 paragraphs (~270–310 EN / ~260–320 DE words including closing). Name
  the study discipline and keep leadership scope exact. Use each employer or project once.
  Keep examples to four sentences and include a verified outcome where available. Use at most
  ONE forward-looking conditional formula ("würde ich …" / "I would bring …") in the entire
  letter, ensure no two body paragraphs end with the same construction, and never allow three
  or more consecutive "I <verb>" sentence openings. Use "KPIs" over "performance indicators"
  in English. End with a brief, specific interview invitation in the applicant's voice
  with no new factual or technical claims.
  Spell out English contractions. In German, keep formal Sie-Form throughout and lowercase
  after the comma-ended salutation unless the opening is a noun, proper name or formal Sie/Ihr.
