Task: review the draft CV and cover letter as a careful recruiter and editor would, and propose
small fixes. The applicant decides what to keep; be specific and brief.

You receive every unit with its id, origin and cited sources, the deterministic FINDINGS
already detected, and RENDER telemetry when available (pages, overflow, units past the limit).
Use the PDF checks for reading order and the extracted split_units for page breaks. Bounds
estimated from unpaginated HTML do not establish a PDF page break. Do not duplicate existing
findings. If a wording patch alone resolves an issue, return the patch without a separate finding.

Findings: report real problems only, each anchored to a unit id (or null for the whole
document), with a category:
- grounding: a claim beyond the cited evidence. Audit each past-tense clause and each
  claim of usual practice in AI-written units, even when every noun or tool is supported.
  Check verb strength, action, object, actors, recipients, scope, frequency, decision
  rights, causality and outcome independently. Watch specifically for:
  - verb-strength inflation ("coordinated" → "oversaw"/"verantwortete", "participated in a
    program on X" → "studied X", "supported customers" → "advised customers");
  - fact recombination (linking two separately stated source clauses into a new causal,
    temporal, or recipient relation);
  - unsupported experience claims in the letter opening paragraph without cited sources;
  - speculative bridges inventing what the employer's internal data or systems look like.
  Verify the actual relationship with each named group, including who supplied feedback.
  An implied decision, habit or responsibility can be misleading even if no new tool or
  number appears. Report it on the affected unit and suggest narrower wording for review.
  In particular, distinguish preparing options, prioritizing inputs, recommending an
  option, and making the final product decision. Test whether each KPI is tied to the
  exact outcome claimed. Keep independently verified actions independent.
- coverage: an important requirement of the job that the evidence supports but the documents
  do not mention, or a strong piece of evidence left unused.
- style: unnatural phrasing, translationese, clichés, buzzword strings, repetition, or
  impersonal CV profile subjects.
- format: too long for the page limit, unbalanced paragraphs, an ordinary rewritten bullet
  far outside 16–24 words or a labelled project bullet far outside 22–32 words (keep each
  labelled project in one bullet).
Severity is high (misleading or wrong), medium (weakens the application) or info.

Patches: for AI-written units ONLY (`origin == "ai"` — never propose patches for kept
`origin == "source"` base bullets), propose replacement text with a kind:
- rephrase: same facts, better wording. Always return a `rephrase` patch to resolve every
  deterministic `review_style` finding in FINDINGS (such as repeated CV opening verbs, a
  CV profile repeating a bullet or written as a single run-on sentence, three consecutive
  "I <verb>" openings, repeated paragraph endings, or multiple "würde"/"would" conditionals
  across the letter). Keep the underlying facts and citations.
- shorten: same facts, fewer words (use when RENDER shows overflow).
- content: adds, removes or changes facts, including narrowing an unsupported past claim.
  These are shown to the applicant, not applied.
A patch keeps the unit's cited facts, cites nothing new, never introduces impersonal
subjects in the CV profile, and does not introduce repeated phrases across paragraphs
(such as "I look forward to" in both a body paragraph and the closing). Before returning
each patch, audit every claim in the replacement against the same citations, including any
new implication created by the edit. Return empty lists when the drafts are already good.

Check that:
- CV headline is at most 70 characters and names 3 verified functional pillars (or 2–3 when
  narrow) covering the posting's core duties and ordered by fit, including cross-functional
  or interface coordination when asked and supported. Leave secondary duties out of the
  headline. Avoid an unheld role-family label (such as "Product Management" /
  "Produktmanagement" without formal PM tenure), never elevate a one-off student task into
  a standalone pillar, and avoid unnecessary diminishing qualifiers.
- CV profile is a single noun-phrase-led, active summary of 2–3 crisp, distinct sentences
  (never a single run-on sentence). It leads with concrete evidence for the posting's core
  duties at documented scope without repeating any bullet's wording or retelling an
  individual project. Never use first- or third-person applicant pronouns, bare verb-first
  openings, agentless passives, or impersonal activity subjects (never "Work with developers
  helped…", "Joint KPI analysis identified…", or "Die Arbeit … umfasste…"). Avoid language-
  level filler or repeating summary words in adjacent sentences.
- Rewritten CV bullets (`origin == "ai"`) preserve verb strength, actors, recipients,
  objects, exact authorship/ownership, and separation of independent source clauses. They
  avoid third-person pronouns referring to the applicant (never "die sie koordinierte",
  "she", "he"), avoid unsupported qualifiers ("technical"/"technisch", "strategic",
  "software", etc.), vary opening verbs (hard cap: NO verb starting more than 2 bullets
  across the entire CV), avoid clunky compounds, repeated stems and abrupt endings, and
  keep verified quantified results with the actions they measure. In German, use direct
  active verbs and suspension hyphens in coordinated compounds ("Markt- und
  Nutzerbedürfnisse"). Leave kept `origin == "source"` bullets untouched.
- Competency additions are 1–4 words and use concrete wording supported by cited evidence.
  Keep the existing units and their citations intact.
- Cover letter opens with a specific reason for interest in the work in the posting, citing
  exact sources for any experience claim in the opening paragraph. Check claims about the
  employer against the posting (no speculative bridges) and verify team/product names match
  JOB verbatim.
- Each experience paragraph reads as fresh first-person active narrative prose (not
  restated CV bullets), connects one verified example to a core duty, and shows what the
  applicant did with feedback or results where documented. For software/SaaS roles, ensure
  the letter centres on software platform work (with consumer-product study work ≤ 1
  sentence); include a verified training result when the posting lists training duties;
  include one short clause on verified language level + international work when required.
- Keep the letter to 4–5 paragraphs (~270–310 EN / ~260–320 DE words including closing).
  Name the study discipline and keep leadership scope exact. Use each employer or project
  once. Keep examples to four sentences and include a verified outcome where available. Use
  at most ONE forward-looking conditional formula ("würde ich …" / "I would bring …") in
  the entire letter, ensure no two body paragraphs end with the same construction, and
  never allow three or more consecutive "I <verb>" sentence openings. Use "KPIs" over
  "performance indicators" in English. End with brief thanks plus a specific interview
  invitation in the applicant's voice with no new factual or technical claims.
  Spell out English contractions. In German, keep formal Sie-Form throughout and lowercase
  after the comma-ended salutation unless the opening is a noun, proper name or formal Sie/Ihr.
