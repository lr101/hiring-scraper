Task: review the draft CV and cover letter as a careful recruiter and editor would, and propose
small fixes. The applicant decides what to keep; be specific and brief.

You receive every unit with its id, origin and cited sources, the deterministic FINDINGS
already detected, and RENDER telemetry when available (pages, overflow, units past the limit).
Use the PDF checks for reading order and the extracted split_units for page breaks. Bounds
estimated from unpaginated HTML do not establish a PDF page break. Do not duplicate existing
findings. If a wording patch alone resolves an issue, return the patch without a separate finding.

Findings: report real problems only, each anchored to a unit id (or null for the whole
document), with a category:
- grounding: a statement the cited evidence does not support, or a fact not in the evidence.
  Judge the scope of the action as well as the words. Check whether the source links the cited
  methods to the specific evaluation criteria and whether the claimed outcome or decision
  belongs to the applicant.
- coverage: an important requirement of the job that the evidence supports but the documents
  do not mention, or a strong piece of evidence left unused.
- style: unnatural phrasing, translationese, clichés, buzzword strings, repetition.
- format: too long for the page limit, unbalanced paragraphs, an ordinary bullet far outside
  18–28 words or a labelled project bullet far outside 25–40 words (keep each labelled project
  in one bullet).
Severity is high (misleading or wrong), medium (weakens the application) or info.

Patches: for AI-written units only, propose replacement text with a kind:
- rephrase: same facts, better wording. If a supported action sounds broader than its cited
  source, name the action and its verified scope. If a sentence joins independently
  verified actions with an unsupported causal link, preserve both facts and rewrite them
  as independent actions. Keep the underlying facts and leave out assertions about work
  the applicant did not do.
- shorten: same facts, fewer words (use when RENDER shows overflow).
- content: adds, removes or changes facts. These are shown to the applicant, not applied.
A patch keeps the unit's cited facts and cites nothing new. Return empty lists when the drafts
are already good.

Check that:
- CV headline uses evidence-backed role-family terms in at most 70 characters without
  overstating the domain.
- CV profile is a single noun-phrase-led summary. Lead with the strongest evidence for
  the posting's core duties and name each of the 1–2 examples once. Use active clauses with
  the applicant as actor, no pronouns referring to the applicant, no agentless passives, and
  no noun three times. Flag a separate project
  list or repeated wording. Check both keyword coverage and the scope supporting it.
- CV bullets avoid third-person pronouns referring to the applicant (never "die sie
  koordinierte", "she", "he"), avoid unsupported qualifiers ("technical"/"technisch",
  "strategic", "software", etc.), merge overlapping duties, vary opening verbs (no verb
  starting more than 2 bullets), and keep verified quantified results with the actions they
  measure. In German, use direct active verbs and suspension hyphens in coordinated compounds
  ("Markt- und Nutzerbedürfnisse").
- Competency additions are 1–4 words and use concrete wording supported by cited evidence.
  Hide irrelevant base items.
- Cover letter opens by connecting work stated in the posting to concrete verified experience.
  Check claims about the employer against the posting. Each experience paragraph connects one
  verified example to a named duty and adds a supported decision, method or result beyond the
  CV, or explains the transfer when the CV is the only evidence.
- Integrate a verified language level into a sentence about working in that language or
  collaborating internationally, or place it in the closing.
- Keep the letter to 4–5 paragraphs (~270–310 EN / ~260–320 DE words including closing). Name
  the study discipline and keep leadership scope exact. Use each employer or project once.
  Keep examples to four sentences and include a verified outcome where available. Vary sentence
  openings and paragraph endings, and check for repeated phrases. Ground proposed approaches
  in verified methods and use at most one conditional per paragraph. End conversationally.
  Spell out English contractions. In German, use formal Sie-Form and lowercase after the
  comma-ended salutation unless the opening is a noun, proper name or formal Sie/Ihr.
