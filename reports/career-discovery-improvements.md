# Career discovery experiments and fixes

**Test date:** 2026-10-05 UTC · **Location study:** Karlsruhe · **Final replay:** `data/career-poc/run-13/`

This report records follow-up experiments against the problems in the initial [career discovery POC](career-discovery-poc.md). The code crawl starts at known company homepages and tries to reach an employer's hiring pages or public job feed. It does not measure the quality of Germany-wide company discovery; the original OSM sample is a small, ordered IT-company sample.

## Tested improvements

| Change or approach | Observed result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Score the destination path and link label, not the hostname; suppress legal, culture and news paths; recognize German “Arbeitgeber” and “Jobangebote” wording | On the unchanged 12-company OSM cohort, useful career content/feed results rose from **6/12** in run-08 to **7/12** in run-12. CodeWrights changed from unresolved to career content: its homepage links to `/de/arbeitgeber`, a hiring page with job offers. In the six-page trivago sample, `/privacy-policy/` and `/life-at-trivago/` no longer consume slots or count as career pages; the crawl reaches actual job and application pages. | Keep. A small, direct gain in both recall and page-budget quality. The 12-company difference is not a general accuracy estimate. [CodeWrights employer page](https://www.codewrights.de/de/arbeitgeber) |
| Skip low-confidence “about” links and do not count redirect aliases as separate pages | Fixture tests confirm that an `/about` link does not displace a strong `/careers` link, and that two aliases to one final URL do not consume two page slots. In the live sample, the main measured benefit was removing weak paths from the shortlist; this did not by itself change the six-page cohort outcome. | Keep. It protects a scarce request/page budget, though a redirect alias still requires an HTTP request to discover its destination. |
| Follow a company-domain Greenhouse application URL carrying `gh_jid` | Trivago's branded `/apply/?gh_jid=…` page exposes its embedded Greenhouse board. The parser then reads the public `trivago` Greenhouse feed with **15 postings**. The same feed is present twice in the seed matrix (company homepage and career-site control), so this is one company/feed, not two discoveries. | Keep. Application routes provide useful provider evidence even where the company site never links a vendor-hosted board directly. |
| Detect and parse a linked Schema.org JSON `DataFeed` | A search-assisted TechniData career-site seed linked to [`jobs.feed.json`](https://karriere.technidata.de/jobs.feed.json). A normal robots-aware GET returned **10 postings**, of which **3** had structured locality `Karlsruhe`. The feed follows Schema.org's [`DataFeed`](https://schema.org/DataFeed) and [`JobPosting`](https://schema.org/JobPosting) types. | Keep as a generic, employer-published feed format. Its use on one tested site is promising, not evidence that it is a universal or stable ATS API. Some location values are absent or only partially describe multi-location jobs. |
| Increase the crawl cap from 6 to 12 pages | On the same 25-seed set, this produced 207 capture records (56 were not in the preceding cache) versus 151 at six pages, but the company outcome counts stayed **9 unresolved, 10 career-content, 6 feed**. It took about 91 seconds with per-origin pacing. | Do not raise the default globally. The extra traffic found no additional outcomes in this test; better ranking or a focused second pass is more efficient. |
| Search-assisted seed discovery | Manual web search surfaced the TechniData career subdomain and CodeWrights' `/de/arbeitgeber` page, neither evident from the initial seed list. Automated search-engine access was not tested. | Useful as a lead-generation stage with recorded query and provenance. For a scheduled system, use a documented search API or another reproducible directory source, then verify each lead by fetching the employer page. |
| Common Crawl index lookup | A targeted query returned 18 historical Trivago career-site URLs, including job/application and non-career URLs. Of six domain checks for unresolved/assisted seeds, one returned MegaCycle's already-known placeholder `/stellenangebote/`; five returned no filtered candidate. Later index calls returned HTTP 502/504, so the test stopped. See [Common Crawl Index](https://index.commoncrawl.org/) and its [getting-started guide](https://commoncrawl.org/get-started). | An optional source of historical URLs and path ideas, not a live company finder or a dependable required dependency. It did not resolve the blocked or missing cases in this sample. |

The 26 rows in run-12 include repeated Trivago seeds and connector/provider controls, so their aggregate counts are not 26 independent companies. In the original 12 OSM rows, run-12 had **5 unresolved, 6 career-content and 1 jobs-feed** result. “Unresolved” means the bounded bot did not verify a page/feed; it does not mean the company has no careers site.

Across distinct parsed `(provider, tenant, feed URL)` combinations in run-12 there were **8 feeds and 89 postings**: Personio 3 feeds/33 postings, Ashby 2/18, Greenhouse 1/15, Lever 1/13, and Schema.org 1/10. Two valid feeds were empty (Cinemo and Ecosia). A successful empty feed is kept distinct from an access or parse failure.

## Access and remaining failure modes

The new TechniData JSON feed was fetched through the same `Client.get` route as HTML, which checks `robots.txt` and each redirect hop. Existing ATS feeds use a separate exact-host/path allowlist for documented public GET endpoints. This experiment did not use browser automation, authentication, proxy rotation, CAPTCHA handling or challenge bypass.

The HTTP sample still includes a robots-related 403, a 429 and network errors. The client permits a missing robots file (404), but treats other unavailable or invalid robots responses conservatively as blocked. [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.pdf) allows a crawler to treat a robots 4xx response as unavailable and access resources, while requiring a complete disallow when robots is unreachable due to server/network failure. We considered loosening the 4xx behavior; this sample did not show that any newly crawled blocked site would yield a useful career result, so the policy was left unchanged. A 429 or an explicit challenge should not be treated as permission to retry aggressively.

Career-page discovery still depends on employer wording and visible links. JavaScript-only pages, cookie gates, login walls, redirects to unrelated job aggregators, inaccessible robots files and sites with no active listings remain difficult. The Softgarden host matcher also saw TechniData's real hosted job route as detection-only; the actual feed came from the site's JSON link. A provider-looking hostname is evidence to inspect, not proof of a usable board.

The parser records job locations but does not geocode them, resolve postal-code/radius matching, distinguish employer-office location from job location, or fully expand multi-location and remote jobs. Karlsruhe's three structured TechniData locations show that location filtering can begin at the listing level, but a Germany-wide location search still needs a separate location normalization step and a clear policy for remote roles.

## Reproducibility and next test

`run-12` contains the live 26-seed result and HTTP metadata: **17 new requests and 146 reused captures**. `run-13` replays all 163 captured responses against the final code with **0 new requests**. Its recorded source hashes match the code. Response bodies remain local and gitignored, as in the original study, so a fresh clone can inspect metadata/results but cannot independently re-parse the captured third-party pages without new requests.

The changes have fixture coverage for German hiring vocabulary, legal-page false positives, redirect deduplication, Greenhouse application evidence, linked Schema.org feeds and hosted-vendor false positives. The next valuable experiment is a larger stratified set across German regions and industries, comparing OSM/directory seeds with documented search results while labeling “career page verified,” “feed parsed,” “jobs matching location,” and “blocked/unresolved” separately. A global page-limit increase is not supported by these results.

## OSM entity → domain → careers association (Mail & Media case)

I tested three association routes on the two nearby OSM records rather than treating proximity as a company match:

| Route | Observation | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Direct `website` tag on the OSM entity | [`node/3718877125`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/3718877125) (“United Internet Media”) has `http://united-internet-media.de`; it redirects to the official HTTPS site. [`node/3705530440`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/3705530440) (“1&1 Mail & Media GmbH”) has no website tag. | Use the tag as the first candidate, keep the OSM source URL as evidence, then prefer a working HTTPS/canonical URL after checking that host's own `robots.txt`. Do not infer a missing domain from nearby tags. |
| Official careers link on the company's own site | The [UIM corporate careers page](https://www.united-internet-media.de/de/unternehmen/jobs-karriere/) links to Mail & Media's shared [job portal](https://www.mail-and-media.com/jobs/). The portal's posting links reveal company-hosted Greenhouse application routes and an EU Greenhouse embed. | Follow exact first-party job/search links ahead of employer-brand pages. Treat a linked group portal as relationship evidence, while retaining the OSM entities as separate records. |
| Curated exact entity-to-group association | The official [1&1 imprint](https://home.1und1.de/impressum/) and [Mail & Media careers page](https://www.mail-and-media.com/jobs/) support a group relationship for the website-less `1&1 Mail & Media GmbH` OSM candidate. They do not justify merging it with the UIM OSM candidate or rewriting either legal name. | Store a source-ID keyed override with `manual_group_match` and an evidence URL. Avoid fuzzy name or distance matching as an automatic join. |

The direct UIM HTTP seed initially failed because the apex robots URL led to HTML rather than a usable robots file. The crawler now tries the same host over HTTPS and, only when that apex policy is unavailable, checks the conventional `www` host's own robots policy. It reached `https://www.united-internet-media.de/de/unternehmen/jobs-karriere/`, followed the visibly linked group job portal, and parsed Greenhouse in **five pages** under the unchanged six-page cap. The Mail & Media homepage path now reaches the `mailmediaportal` feed in **four pages**. Job-detail paths and strong “current openings” links receive priority over generic careers/culture content; explicit ATS application evidence can use one extra link depth without raising the page budget.

The dated Karlsruhe fixture now contains the Mail & Media and UIM records, the domain-association method/evidence, and three live Greenhouse feeds. At observation time they returned **11** rows for `mailmediaportal`, **11** for `mailmediaportal-eng`, and **1** for `uim`. The 23 feed rows contain **17 distinct requisition IDs**; six roles are bilingual duplicates across the first two boards. Thirteen distinct requisitions include a Karlsruhe office. The two language feeds remain stored separately, while job and company views deduplicate bilingual requisitions by Greenhouse `requisition_id`. The UIM requisition is also present in the broader group portal; because the OSM companies remain separate, cross-company deduplication is intentionally not inferred from the shared group site.

The recommender pattern is now direct OSM tag → robots-aware HTTPS/canonicalization → official careers/job-search links → ATS signature or public feed → source-ID keyed reviewed override only when the OSM website is missing. Manual search and public indexes remain useful lead sources, but this case did not need them after the company-side cross-link and official imprint were checked. Evidence pages are linked in the company drawer, and each listed domain shows whether it came directly from OSM or from a verified group-site association.
